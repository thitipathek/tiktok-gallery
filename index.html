How to Read Candlestick Patterns | Candlestick Cheat Sheet | Bullish vs. Bearish Candlestick Pattern Candlestick Patterns: A Guide to Bullish and Bearish Signals Candlestick patterns are a key tool in technical analysis, helping traders identify potential market reversals, continuations, and trends. Originating from Japan in the 18th century, these patterns provide insights into market psychology by visualizing price movements in a structured way. Each candlestick represents a specific time period (e.g., 1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day) and consists of: - Body: Shows the opening and closing prices. - Wicks (Shadows): Indicate the highest and lowest prices during the period. Candlestick patterns are classified as bullish (indicating potential price rises) or bearish (signaling possible declines). *Bullish Candlestick Patterns These patterns suggest a potential upward movement in price, often signaling a reversal from a downtrend or continuation of an uptrend. 1. Hammer - Appearance: Small body with a long lower wick (at least 2x the body). - Location: Forms after a downtrend. - Meaning: Sellers pushed prices down, but buyers regained control, indicating a possible reversal. 2. Bullish Engulfing - Appearance: A small bearish candle followed by a larger bullish candle that "engulfs" the previous candle. - Location: Occurs at the end of a downtrend. - Meaning: Strong buying pressure, signaling a potential trend reversal. 3. Morning Star - Appearance: A three-candle pattern— - First: Long bearish candle. - Second: Small-bodied candle (Doji or spinning top). - Third: Long bullish candle. - Meaning: Indicates weakening selling pressure and a bullish reversal. 4. Piercing Line - Appearance: A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that closes above the midpoint of the first candle. - Meaning: Buyers are stepping in, suggesting a possible uptrend. 5. Three White Soldiers - Appearance: Three consecutive long bullish candles with small wicks. - Meaning: Strong buying momentum, confirming a bullish trend. *Bearish Candlestick Patterns These patterns indicate potential downward price movements, signaling a reversal from an uptrend or continuation of a downtrend. 1. Hanging Man - Appearance: Similar to a Hammer but appears after an uptrend. - Meaning: Sellers are gaining control, warning of a possible reversal. 2. Bearish Engulfing - Appearance: A small bullish candle followed by a larger bearish candle that engulfs it. - Meaning: Strong selling pressure, signaling a potential downtrend. 3. Evening Star - Appearance: A three-candle pattern— - First: Long bullish candle. - Second: Small-bodied candle (Doji or spinning top). - Third: Long bearish candle. - Meaning: Indicates weakening buying pressure and a bearish reversal. 4. Dark Cloud Cover - Appearance: A bullish candle followed by a bearish candle that closes below the midpoint of the first candle. - Meaning: Sellers are overpowering buyers, suggesting a downtrend. 5. Three Black Crows - Appearance: Three consecutive long bearish candles with small wicks. - Meaning: Strong selling momentum, confirming a bearish trend. ICT SMC — Liquidity, BSL, SSL, FVG, BOS, Sweeps & Order Blocks | ICT SMC Liquidity Model ICT SMC — Liquidity, BSL, SSL, FVG, BOS, Sweeps & Order Blocks In ICT/SMC, the main idea is to understand where liquidity is resting, where price is likely to seek it, and how price behaves after taking that liquidity. The important concepts include: BSL • SSL • Liquidity Pools • Liquidity Sweep • BOS • FVG • Order Blocks 1. Buy-Side Liquidity — BSL BSL = Buy-Side Liquidity Buy-side liquidity is generally found above obvious highs. Common BSL locations: - Previous Swing High - Equal Highs — EQH - Previous Day High — PDH - Previous Week High — PWH - Asian High - London High - Session High - Major External High Above these highs, there can be clusters of buy-stop orders and short sellers' stop-losses. Therefore, when price approaches an obvious high, an ICT trader asks: “Is price drawing toward the BSL?” Price may take that liquidity and either continue higher or reject and reverse. 2. Sell-Side Liquidity — SSL SSL = Sell-Side Liquidity Sell-side liquidity is generally found below obvious lows. Common SSL locations: - Previous Swing Low - Equal Lows — EQL - Previous Day Low — PDL - Previous Week Low — PWL - Asian Low - London Low - Session Low - Major External Low Below these lows, there can be clusters of sell-stop orders and long traders' stop-losses. The important question becomes: “Where is the next SSL?” A market may trade lower to take SSL before reversing higher. 3. Liquidity Pools A liquidity pool is an area where orders are likely concentrated. The most obvious pools are usually around: BSL - Equal Highs - Swing Highs - PDH - PWH - Session Highs SSL - Equal Lows - Swing Lows - PDL - PWL - Session Lows The more obvious a high or low is, the more important it can become as a liquidity reference. Instead of asking: “Where is support?” an ICT-style trader may ask: “Where is the liquidity?” Instead of simply looking for resistance, ask: “What buy-side liquidity is resting above this high?” 4. Liquidity Sweep A liquidity sweep occurs when price trades through a known liquidity level and then shows rejection or a change in delivery. Bullish Example Price moves lower → takes SSL → rejects lower prices → displaces higher → breaks structure. This can create a potential bullish setup. Bearish Example Price moves higher → takes BSL → rejects higher prices → displaces lower → breaks structure. 6. FVG — Fair Value Gap FVG = Fair Value Gap An FVG is an imbalance created during strong price displacement. A bullish FVG can form when strong bullish price delivery leaves an area between candle 1 and candle 3 with little or no overlap. A bearish FVG forms in the opposite direction. Bullish sequence: Liquidity → Displacement → Bullish FVG → Retracement → Continuation Bearish sequence: Liquidity → Displacement → Bearish FVG → Retracement → Continuation FVGs are often used as potential retracement or entry areas rather than treating every gap as an automatic trade signal. 7. Order Block — OB An Order Block is commonly identified around the last opposing candle before a strong displacement move. Bullish Order Block A bearish/down candle before significant bullish displacement. Bearish Order Block A bullish/up candle before significant bearish displacement. However, every last opposing candle is not automatically a high-quality Order Block. A stronger OB generally has supporting context such as: - Liquidity taken - Strong displacement - BOS/MSS - FVG formation - Higher-timeframe alignment - Logical premium/discount location The Order Block can then be used as a potential POI — Point of Interest for a retracement entry. Gold Entry Setups | Liquidity, FVG, OB & Breaker | Gold XAUUSD 6 ICT SMC Entry Setups 6 ICT SMC Trade Entry Setups for Gold (XAUUSD) Gold (XAUUSD) can move aggressively, so ICT SMC traders often focus on liquidity, displacement, market structure, and PD Arrays rather than chasing price. 1. Liquidity Sweep + MSS + FVG Identify BSL or SSL near a key high/low. Wait for price to sweep the liquidity. Look for strong displacement in the opposite direction. Confirm MSS on the execution timeframe. Enter on the retracement into an FVG. SL beyond the liquidity sweep. Target opposing liquidity. 2. Order Block + Liquidity Sweep Mark a valid bullish or bearish Order Block. Wait for price to first take nearby liquidity. Look for displacement away from the OB. Enter when price retraces into the OB. SL beyond the OB. Target the next significant liquidity pool. 3. Breaker Block Entry Identify an Order Block that has been violated with displacement. The failed OB can become a Breaker Block. Wait for price to retrace back into the breaker. Look for confirmation on the lower timeframe. SL beyond the breaker. Target opposing liquidity. 4. FVG Retracement Entry Wait for clear displacement. Identify the resulting Fair Value Gap (FVG). Allow price to retrace into the imbalance. Use the CE (Consequent Encroachment) as a refined entry area when appropriate. SL beyond the relevant structure. Target liquidity in the direction of the displacement. 5. BPR Entry Identify overlapping opposing FVGs that create a Balanced Price Range (BPR). Establish directional bias from higher-timeframe context. Wait for price to return to the BPR. Buyside & Sellside Liquidity | Liquidity Pools, Sweeps & Market Structure | ICT SMC Concepts ICT SMC — Important Market Concepts 1. Liquidity Liquidity market mein un areas ko kehte hain jahan large number of orders aur stop losses maujood hote hain. Smart Money aksar price ko in liquidity areas ki taraf move karwati hai. 2. Buyside Liquidity (BSL) Buyside liquidity aam tor par highs ke above hoti hai. Is mein shamil ho sakta hai: Previous Highs Equal Highs Swing Highs Price highs ke upar ja kar buy stops ko target kar sakti hai. 3. Sellside Liquidity (SSL) Sellside liquidity aam tor par lows ke below hoti hai. Examples: Previous Lows Equal Lows Swing Lows Price lows ke neeche ja kar sell-side liquidity ko target kar sakti hai. 4. Fair Value Gap (FVG) Fair Value Gap ek price imbalance hota hai jo strong displacement ke waqt create hota hai. Jab market aggressively move karti hai aur price efficiently trade nahi karta, to beech mein imbalance reh jata hai. Price future mein aksar us area ko revisit karke react ya partially fill kar sakti hai. 5. Liquidity Pool Liquidity Pool ek aisa area hota hai jahan multiple stop losses aur pending orders accumulate hote hain. Equal Highs aur Equal Lows liquidity pools ki common examples hain. Rule: Jahan traders ko obvious stop loss placement nazar aaye, wahan liquidity pool hone ka chance hota hai. 6. Break of Structure (BOS) Break of Structure tab hota hai jab market apni existing structure ko continue karte hue previous significant high ya low ko break kare. Bullish market → Higher High break Bearish market → Lower Low break BOS aksar trend continuation ko confirm karne ke liye use hota hai. 7. Liquidity Sweep Liquidity Sweep tab hota hai jab price kisi obvious high ya low ko temporarily cross karke wahan maujood liquidity ko collect kare. Example: Equal Highs → Price highs ke above sweep → Rejection Lekin sirf liquidity sweep dekh kar entry lena risky ho sakta hai. Confirmation zaroori hai. 8. Order Block (OB) Order Block aam tor par woh last opposing candle ya price area hota hai jahan se strong displacement aur institutional-level move originate hota hai. Bullish Order Block aksar bullish expansion se pehle ka bearish candle/area hota hai, jabke Bearish Order Block bullish expansion se pehle ka bearish candle/area ho sakta hai. Order Block ko akela trade karne ke bajaye structure, liquidity aur displacement ke context mein dekhna better hota hai. 9. Liquidity Sweep + MSS Setup 🔥 Ye ek powerful confirmation-based setup hai: Step 1: Market kisi important liquidity level ko target karti hai. Step 2: Price liquidity ko sweep karti hai. Step 3: Sweep ke baad strong displacement ka wait karein. Step 4: Lower Time Frame par Market Structure Shift (MSS) confirm hone dein. Step 5: Entry ke liye FVG ya Order Block ka retracement use kiya ja sakta hai. Example: Buyside Liquidity Sweep → Bearish MSS → Bearish FVG/OB Retest → Sell Setup Sellside Liquidity Sweep → Bullish MSS → Bullish FVG/OB Retest → Buy Setup Simple Formula: Liquidity → Sweep → Displacement → MSS → FVG/OB Entry → Target Opposing Liquidity 📈 Remember: Context is everything.
